# aevum — brand

> The instance is mortal. The project is not.

The identity is not decoration on top of the protocol — it **is** the argument, in a
form that predates it by four thousand years.

## The root: Minoan Crete

The mark is a **Minoan temple**. In *Gozar el arte, gozar la arquitectura* (Marta
Zátonyi, Ediciones Infinito, ch. "Tierra"), Crete is described as a civilization the
earthquakes destroyed again and again — *"Después de la muerte y la destrucción, Creta,
una y otra vez, se levantó de sus ruinas y empezó nuevamente."* The instance is mortal;
the project rises from its ruins.

Two details from that text are load-bearing here:

1. **Atectonic columns.** Minoan columns are *"más angostos abajo y más anchos arriba"* —
   narrower at the base, wider at the capital. The widened capital is a structural answer
   to earthquakes, and the atectonic form *"simboliza la realidad temible de los
   terremotos"*. The column carries the threat of collapse in its own shape: **precarious
   by design, yet the structure holds and is rebuilt.** Every column in the mark tapers
   this way.
2. **Writing is Crete's legacy.** *"Y el mundo le debe otro maravilloso legado a Creta: su
   escritura."* So the mark is built **out of characters** — a grid of typographic cells
   that form the temple. What survives the mortal executor is what was written down. The
   protocol externalizes memory into written artifacts (baton, round table, lineage); the logo
   says the same thing in stone made of type.

## The mark, two states

The same temple lives in two states, and the animation is the passage between them:

- **Contorno (the mark).** The outline — the plan, the thing that survives *documented*
  (Stuart & Revett drew the Temple of Ilissus before it was destroyed; the drawing
  outlived the building). Used small: favicon, avatar. → `favicon.svg`
- **Living material (the hero).** The temple rendered as shimmering characters that mutate
  while the silhouette never moves — the structure endures, the signs are ephemeral. Used
  large: README header, og image, the web piece. → generated by `temple-motion.html`
  (built in code, not drawn — self-contained, resolution-independent, animatable).

## Palette

Blue-black, cream, blue-grey. No green.

| token     | hex        | use                                  |
|-----------|------------|--------------------------------------|
| `--void`  | `#0A0C10`  | background                           |
| `--ink`   | `#ECE6D6`  | cream — highlights, wordmark         |
| `--silver`| `#9FB2C6`  | blue-grey — mid tones                |
| `--steel` | `#4C6076`  | deep blue-grey — shadow, low cells   |
| `--dim`   | `#6F7C8A`  | muted labels                         |

Cells are colored by luminance: `steel → silver → ink`.

## Type & voice

- **Monospace** everywhere (`ui-monospace, "SF Mono", "JetBrains Mono", Menlo`). A pattern
  in the language of the machine.
- **Wordmark:** `aevum`, lowercase, wide letter-spacing (~0.44em).
- **Lines:** `begins, never ends.` — `The instance is mortal. The project is not.`
  (the second is the first sentence of the README.)

## Assets

- `favicon.svg` — the mark (contorno temple, atectonic). Reads down to 16px.
- `og-cover.png` — social preview (1200×630).
- `temple-motion.html` — the generative hero. `?t=<0..1>` renders one deterministic frame
  (frame capture); `?cell=<px>` overrides the grid (small = crisper). No params = live.
- `pillar-motion.html` — the single-pillar variant (a secondary / loading state).
